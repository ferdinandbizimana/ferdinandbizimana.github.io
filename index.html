Case 01 · Procurement analytics

Rwanda's public tender market in one month

I built a tender monitor from 5,005 Umucyo e-procurement releases. I de-duplicated them to 1,697 tenders and read method and category from the tender reference codes.

Umucyo OCDS releases
1–31 Dec 2024
public data
1,697unique tenders, 196 procuring entities
RWF 542bndisclosed value (≈ USD 393M)
88%of tenders used open bidding
25%of value went single-source (7% of tenders)

What I did

  • Parsed OCDS JSON releases and kept the latest release per OCID
  • Mapped national methods (NCB, ICB, SS, NRT, IRT, RFQ) to OCDS categories
  • Removed 207 undisclosed and 9 placeholder values before analysing value

What it means for a buyer

  • Value sits in a few large tenders, so review effort should follow value, not count
  • Frequent low-value services (28% of tenders, RWF 5–20M) suit framework agreements

Source: Rwanda e-procurement (Umucyo) OCDS releases; Rwanda Tenders Dec 2024 report and Umucyo Tender Monitor (my work).

Case 02 · Procurement risk

Where competition breaks down

The single-bid rate is a standard red flag for weak competition. I measured it by method and checked how many bidders each tender drew.

Same dataset
1,697 tenders
17.6%of tenders received a single bid
3median bidders per tender
200open or restricted tenders still got one bid
RWF 94.7bnsingle-source works across 18 tenders

Recommendations I made

  • Ex-post review of the 18 single-source works tenders (RWF 94.7bn)
  • Target entities with single-bid rates above 30% with wider advertising and simpler qualification
  • Make estimated value and budget mandatory fields; block placeholder amounts

How I use this at RMS

  • The same competition checks guide which tenders go to negotiation
  • Supplier market engagement helped cut missing-supplier cases by about 20%

Source: Umucyo OCDS releases, Dec 2024. Zero-bid tenders (282) were mostly still open at month-end.

Case 03 · Inventory control

Year-end count: from 86.8% to 99.4% SKU accuracy

I led the analysis of a national medical-stock count across 28 storage plants. Every one of 5,156 SAP adjustment lines was reviewed, with no sampling. A line counted as explained only when it quoted a supporting document.

Physical inventory count
30 Jun 2026 · FY2025/26
employer data, anonymised
10,659SKUs counted in 28 plants (~RWF 82bn stock)
99.98%value accuracy after justificationfrom 98.28% raw
97.2%of lines justified, covering 99.4% of value
144open lines, each with an owner and due date

What I did

  • Reconciled MB5B/GL closing stock with counted stock per plant, material and WBS
  • Classified every variance line into 16 root causes from its reason and action text
  • Built the accuracy standard: Good = SKU ≥ 98% and value ≥ 99.5%

What changed

  • Open lines went into a register with owners and a 31 Oct deadline
  • Rule adopted: no justification is accepted without a document number
  • Netting and batch-swap findings fed a corrective action plan for count procedures

Source: RMS Physical Inventory Count Report and Final Variance Investigation Workbook, 30 Jun 2026 (my work). Site names withheld.

Case 04 · Service level

What stockouts cost in lost sales

I measured order fill rate across 906,061 sales-order lines for the year. Value ordered but not delivered is revenue lost, mostly to stockouts. This is the link between better procurement and higher sales.

SAP VA05 order lines
Jul 2025 – Jun 2026
employer data, anonymised
95.7%of ordered value deliveredabove 95% target
90.8%of lines delivered in fullbelow 95% target
RWF 5.2bnordered value not delivered
9.1%of lines received nothing

What I did

  • Joined ordered and delivered quantities per line and priced the shortfall
  • Split results by stock owner, plant and month
  • Ranked plants by lowest fill rate to target replenishment

Why it matters

  • Lost revenue peaked at RWF 940M in March 2026
  • Branch fill rates ranged from 75% to 99%, so the fix is local, not network-wide

Source: RMS Order Fill Rate Workbook FY2025/26 (VA05), summarised in the Variance Investigation Workbook (my work).

Case 05 · Demand & supply planning

Which health programmes are about to run out

For donor-funded programme stock, I compared national stock with average monthly consumption (AMC) for 148 products. Months of stock below 3 flags a stock-out risk.

Programme stock & AMC
stock at 29 Jul 2026
quarterly accountability report
28products at stock-out risk (< 3 months)
33products understocked
50products overstocked
2.7months of stock at central level, value-weighted

What I did

  • Matched SAP materials to the weekly national stock report and AMC
  • Projected the month each product falls below 3 months, using the pipeline
  • Flagged 884 products with no AMC data for regular monitoring

Decision it supports

  • Prioritise emergency orders for at-risk products
  • Slow orders where stock is above the maximum level (RWF 1.9bn)

Source: quarterly programme stock accountability report, end June 2026 (my work).
